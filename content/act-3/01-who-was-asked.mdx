<Briefing title="Population, frame, sample — and what each design licenses" topics={["3.1", "3.2"]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
Three sets, and a study is only as honest as the gaps between them.

1. **The population** is who the conclusion is about. It is fixed by the *claim*, not by the data: the moment a sentence says "the masters of the Lane", the population is the masters of the Lane, whatever list the numbers came off.
2. **The sampling frame** is the list you could actually have drawn from — the schedule, the register, the roll of names at the counter. It is the study's real reach. What is in the population and not in the frame is **undercoverage**; what is in the frame and not in the population is **overcoverage**, and it is just as damaging, because those units answer too.
3. **The sample** is who ended up in the data. It is always a subset of the frame. It can never repair the frame.

That last sentence is the whole module. Enlarging a sample shrinks the *variability* of the estimate around whatever the frame's answer is. It moves the estimate no closer to the population's answer, because the population's answer was never on the list.

Three kinds of study, and three different licences:

- A **sample survey** measures units without disturbing them and is designed to describe a population. If the units were chosen at **random from a frame that covers the population**, the estimate generalizes to that population — and to nothing wider.
- An **observational study** records units as it finds them — grouped by something they already were, like owner class or port of registry. It can show association. It cannot say *because*, because whatever sorted the units into groups may also have sorted the response.
- An **experiment** *imposes* a treatment on units, and if it does so **at random**, the groups differ systematically in nothing but the treatment, so a difference too large to be chance can be attributed to it.

The two randomizations are different things and buy different goods. Random **selection** buys generalization. Random **assignment** buys causation. A study with neither describes the individuals in it and stops there.
</Briefing.Intuition>
<Briefing.Definition>
<Formula tex="s \subseteq F, \qquad \text{undercoverage} = P \setminus F, \qquad \text{overcoverage} = F \setminus P" terms={{ 'P': 'the population — who the conclusion is about', 'F': 'the sampling frame — the list you could have drawn from', 's': 'the sample — who is in the data' }} label="The three sets" />

<Formula tex="\text{coverage} = \frac{|P \cap F|}{|P|}, \qquad \text{response rate} = \frac{|s|}{|\text{contacted}|}" terms={{ '|P \\cap F|': 'population units the frame can reach', '|s|': 'units that gave a usable answer' }} label="Two numbers every method section owes the reader" />
</Briefing.Definition>
<Briefing.Worked>
Your own 2176 return from *Asgard*, {ASGARD_2176.years}: {fmtInt(ASGARD_2176.losses)} losses in {fmtInt(ASGARD_2176.transits)} transits, a rate of {fmtPct(asgardRate, 2)} per transit. That was not a sample of anything. Every scheduled transit in those years was in the file, so the frame *was* the population and the coverage was {fmtPct(1, 0)} — a census, and an observational one: nobody assigned a hull to anything. It licensed exactly one kind of sentence, a description of {ASGARD_2176.years}, and the Directorate's staff tried to read it as a forecast anyway.

Now a case with gaps in it. The Saturn-feeder masters' return, taken the same season: the feeder registry lists {fmtInt(feeder.listed)} masters, of whom {fmtInt(feeder.reachable)} were inside relay reach in the window and could be contacted at all. Coverage is {fmtInt(feeder.reachable)} ÷ {fmtInt(feeder.listed)} = **{fmtPct(feederCoverage, 0)}**: {fmtInt(feeder.listed - feeder.reachable)} masters were not on the list the survey could draw from, whatever they think. Of the {fmtInt(feeder.contacted)} drawn at random from the reachable list, {fmtInt(feeder.answered)} answered — a response rate of **{fmtPct(feederResponse, 0)}** — and {fmtInt(feeder.yes)} of those, **{fmtPct(feederYes, 0)}**, reported a hostile approach.

Write the scope honestly and it reads: *{fmtPct(feederYes, 0)} of responding masters, drawn at random from the {fmtInt(feeder.reachable)} feeder masters inside relay reach.* Not {fmtPct(feederYes, 0)} of the feeder. Now double the draw to {fmtInt(2 * feeder.contacted)}: the interval narrows by about a factor of {fmt(Math.sqrt(2), 2)}, and the {fmtInt(feeder.listed - feeder.reachable)} masters outside relay reach are still outside relay reach.
</Briefing.Worked>
<Briefing.Caution>
*"A big sample fixes a bad frame."* It does not. Sample size and frame defect are unrelated quantities: $n$ controls how tightly the estimates cluster, the frame controls what they cluster **around**. A survey of {fmtInt(doubleCeres)} volunteers at one dock is a more precise measurement of that dock. The only repair for a frame is a different frame.

*Groups are not an experiment.* Comparing masters by owner class, or transits by whether they sailed escorted, is an **observational** comparison no matter how large it is and no matter how clean the arithmetic. The word *because* requires that somebody assigned the treatment, at random, and wrote down how.
</Briefing.Caution>
</Briefing>
