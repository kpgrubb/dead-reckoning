<Briefing title="An experiment is something you did; a study is something you watched" topics={["3.5", "3.6"]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
In an **observational study** you record the groups as you find them. In an **experiment** you *impose* the condition on the units yourself. Only the second can license the word *because*, and only if it is built to.

The vocabulary is exact and the Board's finding gets three of the five words wrong:

- **Experimental units.** The things the treatment is applied to (human subjects, if they are people). One transit, one unit. Count them before anything else.
- **Factor.** An explanatory variable the experimenter sets. **Levels** are its settings. A **treatment** is one combination of levels, one from each factor. Two factors at {fmtInt(themis.levels)} and {fmtInt(dutyLevels)} levels make {fmtInt(themisTreatments)} treatments, not {fmtInt(themis.levels + dutyLevels)}.
- **Response variable.** What you measure afterwards. A column that never varies is not a response. It measures nothing, and no design on earth can compare two groups on it.

Five principles, and a trial that drops one of them drops the conclusion with it:

1. **Comparison.** At least two levels, run over the *same window*, in the *same conditions*. A treated group with no comparison group is an anecdote with a sample size.
2. **Random assignment.** The units are allocated to treatments by a chance device. This is the whole engine: it makes the groups alike, before the treatment, in every variable, including the ones nobody thought of and the ones nobody has a name for.
3. **Control.** Holding the other conditions fixed so that the only thing differing between the groups is the factor. The **control group** is the comparison arm: it may be untreated, or given a placebo, or given the current standard. Control is not a synonym for "nothing".
4. **Replication.** Enough units per treatment that an ordinary difference can be told from a real one, and the whole trial repeatable by somebody else.
5. **Blinding.** Units who do not know their level; assessors who do not know it either. **Double-blind** means both. An *announced* escort is unblinded to everybody on the Lane, including whoever the escort is supposed to deter.

**Confounding** is what happens when principle 2 is missing. A variable is confounded with the factor when it differs between the groups *and* is related to the response, so that their effects arrive together and cannot be told apart. It is not a mistake of arithmetic and no amount of data repairs it: a confounded trial run at ten times the size is the same unanswerable question, asked more expensively.

Three designs, in order of how much you already know:

- **Completely randomized.** Every unit assigned to a treatment purely by chance. Balances everything *on average*, over the runs you did not do.
- **Randomized block.** Sort the units into **blocks**, groups alike in a variable known *before* assignment and related to the response, then randomize separately inside each block. Balance on that variable stops being luck and becomes construction. Block what you can; randomize what you cannot.
- **Matched pairs.** Blocks of two: two units matched on everything you can match on, with the coin decided inside the pair; or one unit measured under both levels, with the *order* randomized. The comparison is made within the pair, so everything the pair shares cancels.
</Briefing.Intuition>

<Briefing.Worked>
The Themis Reach, 2179, and a trial nobody argued about at the time.

**Units:** {fmtInt(themis.transits)} transits. **Factor:** the scrubber cartridge, at {fmtInt(themis.levels)} levels: the Mk 4 and the Mk 2 it replaced. **Response:** off-nominal advisories logged per transit. {fmtInt(themisPerLevel)} transits a level.

The Ceres yard fitted the Mk 4 to whichever hulls came through refit that quarter, and every hull through refit that quarter was working the **outer leg**. So: {fmtInt(themis.outerOnMk4)} of the {fmtInt(themisPerLevel)} Mk 4 transits were outer-leg, and {fmtInt(themis.outerOnMk2)} of the {fmtInt(themisPerLevel)} Mk 2 transits were. Indicator: |{fmt(themis.outerOnMk4 / themisPerLevel, 2)} − {fmt(themis.outerOnMk2 / themisPerLevel, 2)}| = **{fmt(themisGap, 2)}**. The cartridge column and the leg column are the same column, written twice under different headings, and the outer leg carries more debris and longer thermal soaks than the inner. Whatever the advisory counts came out at, the yard could not say which column produced them.

Now count the label-deals. Completely randomized over all {fmtInt(themis.transits)} transits, {fmtInt(themisPerLevel)} to a level: {fmtInt(themisWays)} assignments, every one equally likely. Of those, {fmtInt(themisBlockWays)} put exactly {fmtInt(themisBlockedPerCell)} outer-leg transits on each cartridge. That is **{fmtPct(themisBalanced, 0)}** of them. Chance balances the leg *most runs*, and lands {fmtPct(themisLopsided, 0)} of the time on a split of seven-three or worse.

Block on the leg instead. {fmtInt(themis.legSize)} outer, {fmtInt(themis.legSize)} inner, {fmtInt(themisBlockedPerCell)} of each block to each cartridge, drawn inside the block: now the {fmtInt(themisBlockWays)} balanced assignments are the *only* assignments. Indicator {fmt(themisBlockedGap, 2)}, every run, by construction. You have not made the trial bigger. You have deleted every deal that would have embarrassed it.
</Briefing.Worked>
<Briefing.Caution>
*Random sampling and random assignment are two different dice.* Random **sampling** decides who is in the study, and it buys **generalization**: the right to talk about the population the sample came from. Random **assignment** decides who gets what, and it buys **causation**, the right to say *because*. Neither substitutes for the other. A perfect simple random sample of the Lane, with escorts handed out on request, still cannot say escorts did anything; a lottery run on {fmtInt(BOARD_TRIAL.length)} volunteers can say what the escort did to those {fmtInt(BOARD_TRIAL.length)}, and nothing about anyone else.

*"Control group" read as "untreated group".* Control is the comparison arm, held at a known level of the factor, in the same window, under the same conditions. Sometimes that level is nothing; often it is a placebo or the existing standard. A comparison group drawn from a different month, a different route or a different class of owner is a second study glued to the first, whatever the report calls it.

*Blocking on the response.* A block is formed on a variable known **before** assignment and related to the response: owner, route, drive, hull age, the mark the transit starts from. Group the units by the thing you are about to measure and you have not controlled for it; you have arranged the answer in advance, and the trial will confirm whatever the grouping asserted.
</Briefing.Caution>
</Briefing>

<LogEntry
  title="Random assignment is what lets you say “because”"
  concept="An experiment imposes a treatment on experimental units; the factors are the variables the experimenter sets, their levels combine into treatments, and the response is what is measured afterwards. A column that does not vary is not a response.
