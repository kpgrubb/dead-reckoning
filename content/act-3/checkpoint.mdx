Thirty-three days of down, and the wardroom table still carries the small rubber lip the yard fitted for freefall. You put your knuckles on it out of habit.

The report is on the table in three parts, which is Ebele's idea and a good one. The frame page. The design page. The trial page. Every page carries its displays and then one sentence at the bottom, boxed, big enough to read from the far end of the compartment, because Ferrier told him once that a claim a reader has to hunt for is a claim you are hiding.

He is standing at the end of the table with his hands behind his back. That is new. He has been on this ship eleven weeks and until this morning he sat down to be corrected.

</Scene>

<Scene stamp="MET 080/22:40" location="Bridge · plot" condensed="What the survey bought was testimony. Masters have seen Sulcus hulls turn late, and no survey can put a vector on a turn. What you have bought, in nine days of Solberg's link time and one Board acknowledgement, is testimony.

It is good testimony. Collected from a list you can name, by a method a hostile reader can reconstruct, with the silences written down beside the answers. It says that masters on this Lane have watched Sulcus-flag hulls go off the plotted profile and keep burning.

It is also what people say. Marsh gave you nine profane minutes and every one of them was true and none of it is evidence. A master at 0.9 AU saying *turning late* is a memory of a light through a port. No survey puts eleven degrees and a plume vector on a memory.

To put a number on a turn you have to be there when one happens. At the Mark 9 region, cold, wings folded, the cellar taking the heat, when a Sulcus hull comes through and does whatever it is that they do. You stand at the plot with the mark axis up and work out what that costs, and the arithmetic is all in Sandoval's hands and none of it in yours. Perrine hulls pass Mark 9 about twice in five days. On average.

Torch-off is in three days.

</Scene>
