<Briefing title="Two randomisations, two licences" topics={["3.7"]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
Two different chance devices sit in two different places in a study, and they buy two different things. Confusing them is the most expensive mistake in the subject.

- **Random *sampling*** decides *who is in the study*. It buys **generalization**: the right to talk about the population the sample was drawn from.
- **Random *assignment*** decides *who gets which treatment*. It buys **causation**: the right to say the treatment produced the difference.

Four cells, and each one licenses a different sentence:

| | **random assignment** | **groups as found** |
|---|---|---|
| **random sample** | cause, and it generalizes to the population | association in the population; no cause |
| **units as found** | cause, for these units only | association in these units only |

Now the second idea, and it is the whole of a randomization test. Suppose the escort does nothing at all — not "nothing on average", but nothing to any transit: each hull would have logged the advisories it logged whichever label it drew. Then the labels are **arbitrary**, and the difference you observed is one draw from the set of differences every *other* deal of the labels would have produced. That set is finite. With $n$ units and $k$ of them escorted there are exactly $\binom{n}{k}$ deals, all equally likely by construction — because you made them equally likely with a machine.

Deal them all, record the difference in means each time, and you have the **randomization distribution**: what chance alone produces, given these values and this design. Then ask where the observed difference stands in it. If it stands out at the edge, chance alone is a poor explanation. If it stands in the crowd, chance alone is a perfectly good one, and you have not found anything.

The fraction of deals at least as extreme as the observed difference is the **p-value**, and it is not a single number until you have said **which side**:

- A **directional** claim — *escorts reduce advisories* — is tested one-sided: count only the deals at least as negative as the observed difference.
- A claim that escorts *change* advisories, either way, is two-sided: count deals at least as far from zero in either direction. On a symmetric randomization distribution that is about twice the one-sided figure.

Report the side. A p-value quoted without its side is not a result; it is a number with a factor of two loose in it.

Three more things the p-value is not.

**Significant is not important.** A tiny, useless difference becomes significant if you run enough units; a difference that would change the fleet's escort policy can fail to be significant on twelve transits. Significance is a statement about how easily chance makes a gap this size. Importance is a statement about the world, and only you can make it.

**Not significant is not "no effect".** Failing to find evidence is not finding evidence of none. A trial that could never have detected an effect this small has not tested for it; it has been silent about it.

And a trial cannot see what it cannot generate. If the event you care about happens on a fraction $\pi$ of transits, a trial of $m$ transits produces at least one of them with probability $1 - (1-\pi)^m$ — and if that probability is small, the trial's silence on the matter was decided before it sailed.
</Briefing.Intuition>
<Briefing.Definition>
<Formula tex="d_{\text{obs}} = \bar{x}_{\text{treated}} - \bar{x}_{\text{control}}" terms={{ 'd_{\\text{obs}}': 'the observed difference in group means', '\\bar{x}_{\\text{treated}}': 'mean response of the treated group' }} label="The statistic being tested" />

<Formula tex="p_{\text{one-sided}} = \frac{\#\left\{\, i \,:\, d_i \le d_{\text{obs}} \,\right\}}{\binom{n}{k}}, \qquad p_{\text{two-sided}} = \frac{\#\left\{\, i \,:\, |d_i| \ge |d_{\text{obs}}| \,\right\}}{\binom{n}{k}}" terms={{ 'd_i': 'the difference produced by relabelling i', n: 'units in the trial', k: 'units assigned to the treatment' }} label="The exact randomization p-value" />

<Formula tex="P(\text{at least one loss in } m \text{ transits}) = 1 - (1 - \pi)^m" terms={{ '\\pi': 'probability a single transit ends in a loss', m: 'transits in the trial' }} label="What a trial is able to see at all" />
</Briefing.Definition>
<Briefing.Worked>
*Tindr*'s beacon trial, 2181, run on the Themis Reach by an officer who is now retired and who did it properly.

{fmtInt(tindrEscorted.length + tindrUnescorted.length)} transits; {fmtInt(tindrEscorted.length)} of them given the new approach beacon by coin, {fmtInt(tindrUnescorted.length)} not. Response: off-nominal advisories per transit. Beaconed: {tindrEscorted.join(', ')} — mean {fmt(tindrExact.meanA, 2)}. Not beaconed: {tindrUnescorted.join(', ')} — mean {fmt(tindrExact.meanB, 2)}. Observed difference **{fmt(tindrExact.observed, 2)}**.

There are {fmtInt(tindrWays)} ways for those {fmtInt(tindrEscorted.length)} escort labels to fall among the {fmtInt(tindrEscorted.length + tindrUnescorted.length)} transits — $\binom{n}{k}$ with the numbers put in. Deal every one, take the difference in means each time, and count: **{fmtInt(tindrExtreme)}** of the {fmtInt(tindrWays)} give a difference of {fmt(tindrExact.observed, 2)} or lower. One-sided p = {fmtInt(tindrExtreme)} ÷ {fmtInt(tindrWays)} = **{fmt(tindrExact.pLess, 4)}**. Two-sided, counting the deals as far from zero the other way as well: {fmt(tindrExact.pTwoSided, 4)}.

So on {fmtInt(tindrEscorted.length + tindrUnescorted.length)} transits, chance produces a gap that lopsided about {fmtPct(tindrExact.pLess, 0)} of the time. That is a result. The coin licenses the word *because* for those {fmtInt(tindrEscorted.length + tindrUnescorted.length)} transits — and for nothing else, because the Reach did not hand the officer a random sample of anything.

Now the other arithmetic, which is the one nobody runs before commissioning a trial. Your own {ASGARD_2176.years} return from *Asgard*: {fmtInt(ASGARD_2176.losses)} losses in {fmtInt(ASGARD_2176.transits)} transits, a rate of {fmt(asgardRate, 4)} — about {fmt(1 / asgardRate, 0)} transits to the loss. Take a rounder working figure of {fmtPct(lossRate, 0)} a transit. A trial of {fmtInt(tindrEscorted.length + tindrUnescorted.length)} transits then contains at least one loss with probability {fmtPct(seeOneIn(tindrEscorted.length + tindrUnescorted.length), 1)}; a trial of {fmtInt(bigTrial)} transits, {fmtPct(seeOneIn(bigTrial), 0)}. To be {fmtPct(detectTarget, 0)} sure of seeing even one you need {fmtInt(transitsFor90)} transits at {fmtPct(lossRate, 0)}, and {fmtInt(asgardTransitsFor90)} at *Asgard*'s measured rate. **A trial with losses as its response and a dozen hulls on its roster is not a weak trial. It is not a trial.**
</Briefing.Worked>
<Briefing.Caution>
*"Significant" read as "important".* They are different questions and the answer to one does not constrain the other. A p-value near zero on ten thousand transits can attach to a difference of two hundredths of an advisory, which no captain would spend a cutter on; and a difference worth the whole escort budget can sit in the middle of a randomization distribution built from twelve hulls. Report the **size** of the effect next to the p-value, always, and let the reader do the second job.

*"There is a difference, so it is real".* Two groups of six will differ. They will differ if you sort them by hull name. The randomization distribution exists precisely to say how big a difference the deal alone routinely makes, and until the observed gap is compared against it, "there is a difference" carries no information whatever.

*"No evidence" read as "no effect".* A non-significant result says the data are consistent with chance. It does not say the treatment does nothing, and it never says the treatment does nothing *to a thing the trial did not measure*. A trial whose response is advisories has said nothing about losses, however many transits it ran.

*A cause from an observational study.* No amount of care with the analysis converts groups-as-found into an experiment. If the units were not assigned by a chance device, the strongest honest word is *associated*, and the sentence needs the lurking variables named beside it.
</Briefing.Caution>
</Briefing>

Solberg logs one more thing before he goes off watch, because he logs everything. A traffic query from the Uruk office, timestamped forty minutes after *Asgard*'s escort list went out, asking on what authority Service cutters on the Lane are being allocated by a Compact hull. He reads it verbatim, notes the time, and files it without comment.

Ferrier reads it, and then reads the paragraph number back into the log a second time, with the date.

"Captain Ostrow will make something of that in due course," she says. "Let him. The authority is real, it is written down, and the draw is on a sheet with every discarded pair on it. There is nothing here for him to find, which will not stop him looking, and the looking will be in the file too."

</Decided>

<Decided id={DECISION_IDS.lottery} is="decline">

Nobody at Uruk asks a question, because nothing was ordered and *Asgard*'s master tossed her own coin on her own account. Ferrier notes that too, in the same flat hand: *authority available under paragraph eleven, not exercised; analysis conducted on the cutter's own assignment.* It is a cleaner file and a smaller one, and she does not say which she would have preferred.

</Decided>

So: you cannot say escorts work. You cannot say they do not. What you can say, and what nobody on the Lane has said in writing before, is exactly how much a trial of this size is able to see — which is the first honest sentence anybody has written about escort policy in two years, and it closes the subject rather than opening it.

Which leaves the thing the testimony kept pointing at and never reached. Independent masters saw Perrine hulls turn late on the approach. The Register is honest about *when* and *where* and will not say *why*. Escorts are not the explanation for anything and were never going to be, because nobody has yet asked the only question that has an answer in it: not who was attacked, and not who was protected, but **where the hulls actually go**.

The mark axis has been sitting on the plot since Act I with thirty-one marks on it, and it has stopped looking like a list.
