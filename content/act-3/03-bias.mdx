<Briefing title="Bias is a direction; variability is a spread" topics={["3.4"]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
Imagine repeating the whole survey a thousand times under the same method. You get a thousand estimates. That cloud of estimates has a **centre** and a **width**, and the two are damaged by completely different things.

**Sampling variability** is the width. It comes from the luck of the draw and nothing else, it is what a standard error measures, and it shrinks as $1/\sqrt{n}$. It is not an error in the method; it is the price of not asking everybody, and it is *quantifiable*.

**Bias** is the distance from the centre of the cloud to the true value. It comes from the method, it is the same for every repetition, and **it does not shrink with $n$**. A biased design run at $n = 1000$ is the same wrong answer, held more firmly.

The named mechanisms, each with a direction you can usually predict before you collect anything:

- **Undercoverage.** The frame misses part of the population. Direction: toward whatever the reachable part believes. A dock-office frame over-weights whoever docks.
- **Convenience sampling.** Units chosen because they were easy. A special case of undercoverage with no frame at all and no computable error.
- **Voluntary response.** The units decide whether to be in the sample. Direction: toward strong opinion, and usually toward grievance, because a person with a complaint will spend fifteen minutes on a form and a person without one will not.
- **Nonresponse.** You selected them properly and could not get an answer. Direction: toward whatever the *responders* believe — which is a real and estimable quantity, and not the one you set out to measure.
- **Response bias.** The answer differs from the truth because of how it was asked: **wording** that names the conclusion before the question ("*Do you believe pirates … are responsible?*"), an **interviewer** the respondent wants to please or fears, a question about something the respondent is unwilling to admit. Direction: toward the socially safe answer, or toward whatever the question suggested.

Random selection is a cure for exactly one disease. It makes the selection unbiased *within the frame*. It does nothing about a bad frame, nothing about who refuses to answer, and nothing whatever about a leading question, because it does not change a word of it.

And there is a harder case, which is this survey. When a whole stratum will not answer anybody, a probability design does not estimate what the frame believes. It estimates, without bias, what the **responding part of the frame** believes — and it measures the size of the silence. Those are two parameters, and the only dishonest report is the one that quotes the first number and calls it the second.
</Briefing.Intuition>
<Briefing.Definition>
<Formula tex="\text{bias} = E[\hat{p}] - p, \qquad \operatorname{SD}(\hat{p}) = \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}" terms={{ 'E[\\hat{p}]': 'the centre of the estimates over repeated sampling', 'p': 'the parameter the claim is about', 'n': 'the sample size' }} label="Centre and width are different quantities" />

<Formula tex="\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\text{bias}^2 + \operatorname{Var}(\hat{p})} \;\xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{}\; |\text{bias}|" terms={{ '\\text{RMSE}': 'typical total distance from the truth', '\\operatorname{Var}': 'the squared spread, which goes to zero' }} label="Why a bigger biased sample does not help" />

<Formula tex="\text{nonresponse rate} = \frac{\text{contacted} - \text{answered}}{\text{contacted}}" terms={{ '\\text{contacted}': 'units the design selected and reached for', '\\text{answered}': 'units that gave a usable response' }} label="The number that must be printed beside the estimate" />
</Briefing.Definition>
<Briefing.Worked>
Your own ship, 2176, and a case where the truth came out afterwards. *Asgard* carried {fmtInt(asgardCrew.ratings)} ratings. The question was whether a rating had been through a sink alarm in the commission, and because the maintenance log recorded every alarm by compartment, the answer was knowable later: **{fmtInt(asgardCrew.sinkAlarms)} of {fmtInt(asgardCrew.ratings)}**, a true share of {fmt(asgardTruth, 3)}.

The first attempt was a notice in the mess. {fmtInt(asgardCrew.volunteers)} ratings filled it in; {fmtInt(asgardCrew.volunteerYes)} said yes — {fmt(asgardVolunteerP, 3)}. Bias: {fmt(asgardVolunteerP, 3)} − {fmt(asgardTruth, 3)} = **{fmt(asgardBias, 3)}**, and the direction was predictable before a single form came back, because a rating who had been sealed in a hot compartment wanted to say so and a rating who had not had nothing to write.

The second attempt was an SRS of {fmtInt(asgardCrew.srsN)} off the watch bill, twice, a week apart: {fmtInt(asgardCrew.srsYesA)} yes and then {fmtInt(asgardCrew.srsYesB)} yes — {fmt(asgardSrsA, 3)} and {fmt(asgardSrsB, 3)}, on either side of {fmt(asgardTruth, 3)}. The two estimates differ by {fmt(Math.abs(asgardSrsA - asgardSrsB), 3)} and neither is wrong: the standard deviation of an estimate at this size is {fmt(asgardSe, 3)}, so that is exactly the wobble the design promises.

Now the arithmetic that decides everything. Quadruple the mess notice to {fmtInt(4 * asgardCrew.volunteers)} volunteers and its spread halves, to about {fmt(asgardSeBig, 3)} — while its bias sits, unmoved, at {fmt(asgardBias, 3)}. Typical total distance from the truth: {fmt(asgardRmse, 3)}, which is {fmtPct(asgardSeBig / asgardRmse, 0)} spread and the rest pure direction. Against an SRS of {fmtInt(asgardCrew.srsN)} whose typical distance is the {fmt(asgardSe, 3)} above and whose centre is the truth. **The small honest sample beats the large dishonest one by a factor of {fmt(asgardRmse / asgardSe, 1)}, and the gap widens every time you spend more money on the wrong design.**
</Briefing.Worked>
<Briefing.Caution>
*Bias fixed by $n$.* The commonest error in the subject, and the one the Ensign made out loud in the wardroom. More data narrows the cloud; it does not move it. If you cannot say which direction a mechanism pushes, you cannot correct for it either — you can only report it.

*Variability called bias.* Two honest samples of the same population give different numbers. That is not evidence that either is biased, and a reviewer who calls a difference of one standard error "a discrepancy" is reading noise as signal. Bias is a statement about the **centre of the repeated-sampling distribution**, which no single sample displays.

*Random selection as a cure for response bias.* Choosing respondents by digits does not unask a leading question. Q4 — "{CERES_Q4}" — names the conclusion, the culprits and their home port before it asks anything, and it would do so just as efficiently to a perfect simple random sample of the corridor.
</Briefing.Caution>
</Briefing>
