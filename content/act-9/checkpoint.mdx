<LogEntry
  title="Act IX — a slope is a statistic, and a case is judged one finding at a time"
  concept="Inference for a slope rests on the population regression model μy = α + βx with constant σ, under which the fitted slope b is a statistic: centred at β, approximately Normal, with SD σ/(σx·√n), estimated in practice by SE(b) = s/√Sxx. Every procedure about β runs on df = n − 2 because two parameters were estimated to place the line, and every condition — Linear, Independent, Normal residuals, Equal SD, Random — is judged on the residuals or on the design, never on a graph of the response. The interval b ± t*·SE(b) estimates the rate and settles a claim about any stated value by whether that value lies inside it; the test t = (b − β₀)/SE(b) holds the slope against a null that is zero when the question is whether a relationship exists and is whatever the science fixes when the rate itself is disputed. Computer output always prints a two-sided test against zero, so a one-sided question halves that P only when b falls on the alternative's side, and a non-zero null cannot use the printed t at all. A finding carries its procedure, its conditions, its conclusion in context and its limit; a body of findings is judged one at a time and then on whether one innocent account explains all of them together."
