<Briefing title="Two ways to be wrong, and the price of each" topics={["6.7"]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
A significance test is a decision rule, and a decision rule run honestly still makes mistakes. Not careless ones: mistakes the procedure *will* make at a rate that can be computed in advance and, in one case, chosen.

There are exactly two, because there are two ways the world can be and two things the test can say.

|  | $H_0$ is true | $H_0$ is false |
|---|---|---|
| **reject $H_0$** | **Type I error**, probability $\alpha$ | correct, probability **power** |
| **fail to reject** | correct | **Type II error**, probability $\beta$ |

**A Type I error is a false alarm.** The world is quiet and the test says it is not. Its probability is $\alpha$, and $\alpha$ is *set* by whoever writes the test, before the data arrive. That is what makes the number interesting: it is the one error rate in the procedure that is a decision.

**A Type II error is a miss.** Something is there and the test does not report it. Its probability is $\beta$, and nobody sets $\beta$. It falls out of three things: how strict the cutoff is, how much data there is, and how big the thing being looked for actually is. It can be influenced. It cannot be named.

**Power is $1 - \beta$**, the probability that the test *does* report a difference that is genuinely there. Power is a property of the test **and** a specific alternative **and** the sample sizes. "What is the power of this test" is never a complete question. Power against *what*?

Three things move it, and it is worth knowing which of the three costs money.

- **Sample size.** More data narrows both sampling distributions, so the same real difference sits further apart in standard errors and power rises. This is the only lever that improves both error rates at once, and the only one that cannot be pulled by making a decision. It costs seasons.
- **The significance level.** Lowering $\alpha$ slides the cutoff away from the null, which is what it is for, and drags power down behind it. Fewer false alarms, more misses, always, in that proportion. No setting of $\alpha$ reduces both.
- **The size of the true difference.** A test can have excellent power against a difference anybody would call an emergency and almost none against one half that size. Nothing about the test changed; the target moved.

Which brings the argument back to $\alpha$. Because $\alpha$ is a choice and $\beta$ is a consequence, setting $\alpha$ is a statement about **which of the two mistakes you would rather make**, and it is defensible only if it was made before the data with both consequences written down beside it.
</Briefing.Intuition>
<Briefing.Definition>
<Formula tex="\alpha = P(\text{reject } H_0 \mid H_0 \text{ true})" terms={{ '\\alpha': 'the significance level: the Type I error rate, chosen in advance', 'H_0': 'the null hypothesis: no effect, no difference' }} label="Type I error: a false alarm" />

<Formula tex="\beta = P(\text{fail to reject } H_0 \mid H_0 \text{ false})" terms={{ '\\beta': 'the Type II error rate: not chosen, but consequent', '\\text{fail to reject}': 'the test comes back short and nothing is reported' }} label="Type II error: a miss" />

<Formula tex="\text{power} = 1 - \beta = P(\text{reject } H_0 \mid \text{a specific alternative is true})" terms={{ '\\text{power}': 'the chance of catching what is there, always against a stated alternative', '1 - \\beta': 'the complement of the miss rate, NOT of the false-alarm rate' }} label="Power" />

<Formula tex="\hat p^\star = p_0 + z^\star \sqrt{\frac{p_0(1 - p_0)}{n}} \qquad \text{power} = P\left(Z > \frac{\hat p^\star - p_A}{\sqrt{p_A(1 - p_A)/n}}\right)" terms={{ '\\hat p^\\star': 'the cutoff: how extreme the estimate must be before the test rejects, computed under H₀', 'z^\\star': 'the critical value for the chosen α', 'p_A': 'the specific alternative the power is measured against' }} label="Computing power: the cutoff under H₀, the area under Hₐ" />
</Briefing.Definition>
<Briefing.Worked>
The Themis Reach survey office, 2182: a review that was planned properly and then not funded.

The Reach posts a hold rate of {fmt(themisP0, 2)}, the proportion of arrivals held at the mark, and three masters have complained that the real figure is worse. The office proposes a review of {fmtInt(themisN)} arrivals, testing H₀: p = {fmt(themisP0, 2)} against Hₐ: p greater than {fmt(themisP0, 2)}, and before committing the berth time it asks the sensible question: *if the true rate really were {fmt(themisPA, 2)}, would this review find it?*

**The cutoff, under $H_0$.** At $\alpha = 0.05$, one-sided, with a critical value of 1.645:

<Formula tex={themisCutoffTex} terms={{ 'p_0': 'the posted rate: the null value', 'n': 'arrivals in the review' }} label="How high the sample rate has to come before the office rejects" />

**The area beyond it, under $H_a$.** If the true rate is really {fmt(themisPA, 2)}, the sample proportion is centred there with standard error {fmt(themisSeA, 5)}, so

<Formula tex={themisPowerTex} terms={{ 'p_A': 'the alternative the power is measured against' }} label="Power against a true rate of 0.16" />

**Power {fmt(themis05.power, 3)}**, and therefore β = {fmt(themis05.beta, 3)}. Run this review on a Reach where the hold rate really is {fmt(themisPA, 2)} and it comes back with nothing about one time in three.

Now move each lever in turn.

- **Tighten $\alpha$ to 0.01** and hold everything else: power falls from {fmt(themis05.power, 3)} to **{fmt(themis01.power, 3)}**. Nothing about the Reach changed. The office made its own test harder to trigger and paid {fmt(themis05.power - themis01.power, 2)} of detection rate for it.
- **Quadruple the review** to {fmtInt(4 * themisN)} arrivals, at the *stricter* $\alpha = 0.01$: power **{fmt(themisBig.power, 3)}**. More data buys back everything the stricter level cost and a good deal more, which is why the honest answer to "we need to be more rigorous" is usually "then we need a bigger file".
- **Halve the difference** being looked for, a true rate of {fmt(themisSmallPA, 2)} instead of {fmt(themisPA, 2)}, at the original $\alpha = 0.05$: power **{fmt(themisSmallEffect.power, 3)}**. Same review, same level, same arithmetic, and it is now very nearly blind. The target got smaller.

The Reach did not get its funding. The review was run on the arrivals the office already had, it came back short, and the file was closed with the words *no evidence of an elevated hold rate*.
</Briefing.Worked>
<Briefing.Caution>
*"A Type I error is always the worse error."* It is worse in a courtroom, where the whole procedure is built around the cost of convicting the innocent and where the vocabulary of statistics was borrowed from. It is not worse in a pressure-seal inspection, where a false alarm costs a week and a pallet of stock and a miss costs a compartment with people in it. Naming both prices, in context, is the entire analysis; anything less is a definition.

*"Power = 1 − α."* The most common error in the topic, and the most expensive, because it makes the whole calculation look unnecessary. $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are computed on **different sampling distributions**: $\alpha$ is a tail of the one $H_0$ predicts, $\beta$ is a tail of the one the alternative predicts. There is no reason for them to be complements and they are not. In the Reach review, $1 - \alpha = 0.95$ and the power was {fmt(themis05.power, 3)}. The sum that *is* 1 is $\beta + \text{power}$, because those two share a condition.

*"A smaller α is free, or at least cheap."* It is a purchase, and the currency is power. Every point of false-alarm rate bought back is paid for in misses, and the exchange rate depends on how big the difference is: against a large difference the stricter level costs little, against a small one it can cost everything. Quote the price when you quote the level.

*"We failed to reject, so H₀ is true."* A Type II error is by definition the case where $H_0$ is false and the test did not say so. Failing to reject leaves $H_0$ standing as an unrefuted possibility and never establishes it, and a low-powered test that missed a real difference produces exactly the same page as a corridor where nothing is wrong. *No evidence of an effect* and *evidence of no effect* are different sentences, and only one of them is ever available.
</Briefing.Caution>
</Briefing>
