Ebele offers this one at the sensor bay door, on a slate, unasked. You do not need it to work the watch. It is here because the curve on the sensor display is doing something you already know how to do, under a name you were taught in a different room.

A normal model is a **density**: a curve whose height $f(x)$ is not a probability. Probability is the **area** the curve encloses over an interval — and area under a curve is the definite integral.

<Formula

  terms={{ 'f(x)': 'the density — the height of the curve at x, never itself a probability', 'a, b': 'the two cutoffs bounding the interval', '\\int_a^b': 'the area the curve encloses between them' }}
  label="Probability is a definite integral of the density"
/>

**Where the integral comes from.** Cut $[a, b]$ into $n$ strips of width $\Delta x = (b - a)/n$. Over one strip the curve barely moves, so a rectangle of that width and the curve's height at the strip's midpoint is close to the area over it. Add them:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i)\,\Delta x \;\xrightarrow[\;n \to \infty\;]{}\; \int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$$

That sum is a Riemann sum, and the integral is its limit. Nothing new is introduced at the limit; the rectangles simply stop being wrong. Eight of them get the third decimal; two hundred get the sixth.

**Why a single value has no probability.** Ask for the area over one point rather than an interval. Then $a = b$, every rectangle has width zero, and the sum is zero however many you take:

<Formula
  tex="P(X = x) = \int_x^x f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t = 0"
  terms={{ 'x': 'one exact value on the axis', '\\int_x^x': 'an interval of width zero — no area, at any height' }}
  label="A density assigns no probability to a point"
/>

So for a continuous model $P(X \ge x)$ and $P(X > x)$ are the same number, and "at most" and "less than" agree. The height $f(x)$ can be anything — it can exceed 1 — because it is probability *per unit of x*, and only multiplying it by a width turns it into probability at all. A signature of exactly 109.2 percent has probability zero; a signature of 109.2 percent *or hotter* does not.

**And the total is one.** Over the whole axis the hull is somewhere:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = 1$$

which is why an upper tail and everything below it must add to 1, and why you may compute either one and subtract.

Add rectangles below and watch the midpoint sum close on the exact area. Then collapse the interval end onto its start: the width goes to zero, and the area goes with it.

**Why this matters here.** Every number the normal model returns this watch — the proportion of honest hulls above the flagged ratio, the 68, the 95, the 99.7 — is one of these integrals, evaluated for you. Knowing that it is an area and not a height is the difference between reporting a probability and reporting the height of a curve, which is not a probability and never was.
