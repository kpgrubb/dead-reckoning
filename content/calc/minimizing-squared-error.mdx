"Least squares" is not a convention. It is the answer to a minimisation problem, and the derivative is what answers it.

Fix the data. Let a candidate line be $\hat{y} = a + bx$. Every choice of $(a, b)$ has a total cost — the sum of the squared vertical misses:

<Formula
  tex="S(a, b) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(y_i - a - b x_i\bigr)^2"
  terms={{ 'S(a, b)': 'total squared residual for this line', 'y_i - a - b x_i': 'the i-th residual: actual minus predicted', 'n': 'number of points' }}
  label="The cost of a candidate line"
/>

$S$ is a smooth function of two numbers. At its lowest point both partial derivatives are zero.

**Differentiate in $a$ first.** Only the constant moves:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = -2\sum \bigl(y_i - a - b x_i\bigr) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum \bigl(y_i - a - b x_i\bigr) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \bar{y} = a + b\bar{x}$$

The residuals sum to zero, and the line passes through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$. That is not a rule someone imposed; it is what setting one derivative to zero forces.

**Now differentiate in $b$.** Each term carries its own $x_i$ through the chain rule:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = -2\sum x_i \bigl(y_i - a - b x_i\bigr) = 0$$

Substitute $a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x}$ and the sums collapse to deviations from the means:

<Formula
  tex="b = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2} = \frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}} = r\,\frac{s_y}{s_x}"
  terms={{ 'S_{xy}': 'sum of products of deviations', 'S_{xx}': 'sum of squared deviations in x', 'r': 'correlation', 's_y / s_x': 'ratio of standard deviations' }}
  label="The least-squares slope"
/>

The formula in the briefing is a derivative set to zero, rearranged.

**Why it is a minimum and not a maximum.** Hold the intercept at its best value for each slope. Then $S$ is a quadratic in $b$ with a positive leading coefficient $S_{xx} = \sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 > 0$ — an upward parabola. Its second derivative $\partial^2 S / \partial b^2 = 2S_{xx}$ is positive everywhere, so the one place the tangent goes flat is the bottom. There is exactly one least-squares line, and no local minimum to get trapped in.

Drag the slope below. The curve is $S$; the amber line is its tangent; the readout is $\mathrm{d}S/\mathrm{d}b = -2(S_{xy} - b\,S_{xx})$. Find where the tangent is flat and you have found $b$.

**Why this matters here.** The instrument in this module draws each residual as a literal square and asks you to shrink the total area — you are doing, by hand, the minimisation the derivative solves in one line. When the fit snaps to slope and intercept, it is not choosing a line that looks right; it is landing on the single point where both partial derivatives vanish.
