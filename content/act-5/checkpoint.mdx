<Scene stamp="MET 118/06:20" location="Wardroom" condensed="You write the sentence that closes the Act: the rate argument depends on which null the Board is allowed to choose; the composition argument does not. The nineteen are not noise. What is still missing is a hypothesis, a test, a p-value, and a sentence you would sign in front of a board of inquiry — which is what Ceres is for.">

You write it in two lines, because two is what it takes.

*A comparison of the Lane's loss rate against a baseline is only as good as the baseline, and the baseline is the Board's to choose. A comparison of one owner's hulls against everyone else's on the same corridor, in the same file, over the same six years, is not.*

That is the whole of it. The composition argument — these hulls against those hulls, inside one Ledger — needs nobody's permission to pick a corridor, because the corridor is the same on both sides of the comparison.

And against the only thing the Board's own file offers as a picture of honest variation, the nineteen do not fit inside it. A mean of nineteen honest transits has a standard deviation of {fmt(SE_MEAN_19, 2)} days. Theirs is not {fmt(SE_MEAN_19, 2)} days out. It is several times that, and there is no honest arrangement of the Lane in which that is ordinary.

What you do not have is everything that turns a finding into a document the Service is obliged to answer. A hypothesis, stated before the arithmetic. A test with a name. A number that says how often chance alone would do this. And one sentence, in context, that you would sign with your hull number under it in front of a board of inquiry.

Ceres has the register with the beneficiaries in it. Ceres has an adjuster who has paid somebody two point one billion and would like to know why the Compact navy is only now curious.

Fifteen days.

</Scene>
