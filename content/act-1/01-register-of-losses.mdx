<Briefing title="Individuals, variables, and a table that stands for a process" topics={["1.1", "1.2", "1.3"]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
Before any display: an **individual** is the thing one row describes. A **variable** is something recorded about each individual, and it takes one of a few kinds.

A **categorical** variable places the individual in a group. `classification` takes the values *accident*, *piracy* and *unknown*; the three have no order and no arithmetic, and *piracy* is not more than *accident*. `office` and `owner_class` work the same way.

A **quantitative** variable measures or counts the individual, and splits again. **Discrete** is a count: dropouts filed in a year. **Continuous** is a measurement on a scale, where any value in an interval is possible. `mark_last` is a fractional position on a twelve-mark corridor, so Mark 9.35 is a real place; `time_to_silence` is elapsed hours; `hull_age` is elapsed years.

An **identifier** names the individual and is not a variable at all. `record_id` and `hull` label the row. No summary of an identifier means anything.

The trap is a category written with digits. Office 01 and office 02 are two names. Severity coded 1, 2, 3 is three names. You can compute their mean, and the mean will be a number, and the number will be about nothing.

For a categorical variable a **frequency table** gives the count in each category; dividing each count by the total gives **relative frequencies**, which sum to 1 across the categories of one variable. Which total is the whole question. A share *among all thirty-one losses* and a share *among the losses one office classified* are different numbers answering different questions, and only the second is about that office.

Last, and this part is not arithmetic. The Register's individuals are *incidents as recorded and classified by two offices*. It is a recorded sample of a reporting process, not of the corridor. Every row in it passed through somebody's judgement on the way in, and so did every row that is missing.
</Briefing.Intuition>
<Briefing.Definition>
<Formula tex="\text{relative frequency of category } k = \frac{n_k}{n}, \qquad \sum_k \frac{n_k}{n} = 1" terms={{ 'n_k': 'count of individuals in category k', 'n': 'the denominator — every individual the table describes, after every filter' }} label="Relative frequency" />

A **conditional** relative frequency restricts the denominator to one group: the share of category $k$ among the individuals in row $i$ is $n_{ik} / n_{i}$, where $n_i$ is that row's total.
</Briefing.Definition>
<Briefing.Worked>
The Lane Authority's sister file for the Mars–Belt corridor, 2170–76, records {marsBeltTotal} losses with the same three codes: {marsBelt.map((r) => `${r.value} ${r.count}`).join(', ')}.

Relative frequencies, each count over {marsBeltTotal}: {marsBelt.map((r) => `${r.value} ${fmt(r.relFreq, 3)}`).join(', ')}. They sum to {fmt(marsBelt.reduce((s, r) => s + r.relFreq, 0), 3)}, as the relative frequencies of one variable's categories always do. That sum is the cheapest check there is on a table. In the Mars–Belt file *accident* carries {fmtPct(marsBelt[0].relFreq, 0)} of the losses and *unknown* {fmtPct(marsBelt[2].relFreq, 0)}: a corridor whose offices mostly reached a finding.
</Briefing.Worked>
<Briefing.Caution>
**A coded number treated as a measurement.** Data exports ship `office` as 01 and 02 and `severity` as 1, 2, 3. A mean of 1.4 is arithmetic performed on names. Test any column by asking whether its codes could be swapped for letters without losing anything. If they could, the variable is categorical.

**Relative frequencies that do not sum to 1.** When they miss, either a row has been dropped or the denominator has changed: a filter left on, a category with no records omitted from the table, a "not classified" row deleted. Add them up every time. The sum is a free audit of the denominator.

**The conditional denominator.** "What share of the losses are Uruk and *unknown*" divides by every loss in the file. "What share of Uruk's losses are *unknown*" divides by Uruk's row total. The numerator is the same cell both times; only the second sentence is about Uruk.
</Briefing.Caution>
</Briefing>

<Calculator.Step keys={['STAT', '1:Edit']}>Type one group's counts into **L1**, one category per row. Every category goes in, including any with a count of zero; a category left out changes the total.</Calculator.Step>

<Calculator.Step keys={['2nd', 'LIST', '▸ MATH', '5:sum(']} note="sum(L1)">The denominator, computed rather than copied. `L1` is `2nd` `1`.</Calculator.Step>
<Calculator.Read label="sum(L1)" value={String(marsBeltTotal)}>the Mars–Belt file's {marsBeltTotal} losses — the total the whole table divides by.</Calculator.Read>
<Calculator.Step note="L1/sum(L1) STO▸ L2">Divide the list by the number: one line turns the whole column of counts into relative frequencies. `STO▸` is the key above `ON`.</Calculator.Step>
<Calculator.Read label="L2(1)" value={fmt(marsBelt[0].relFreq, 4)}>the share coded *{marsBelt[0].value}* — {marsBelt[0].count} of {marsBeltTotal}.</Calculator.Read>
<Calculator.Watch>**Check `sum(L2)` reads 1.** If it does not, a category is missing from L1 or one has been entered twice, and every proportion in L2 is wrong.</Calculator.Watch>
<Calculator.Watch>**A two-way table is two lists, and two totals.** Put one office's row in `L1` and the other's in `L2`. The conditional distribution of the first office is `L1/sum(L1)`; of the second, `L2/sum(L2)`. Dividing either row by `sum(L1)+sum(L2)` gives a *joint* share — what fraction of the whole file is that office and that code — which is a different question and a smaller number. Decide which sentence you are writing before you press the division key.</Calculator.Watch>
<Calculator.Watch>Two rows of different length cannot be compared on `L1` against `L2`. Counts are the wrong scale: compare `L1/sum(L1)` against `L2/sum(L2)`, and print both row totals beside them.</Calculator.Watch>
</Calculator>
