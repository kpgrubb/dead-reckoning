import { losses, timeToSilence } from '@/instruments/act-1/data'
import { CheckpointGate } from '@/instruments/act-1/CheckpointGate'

export const gradual = timeToSilence.filter((t) => t >= 1).length
export const abrupt = timeToSilence.length - gradual
export const sulcusGradual = losses.filter((l) => l.time_to_silence >= 1 && l.hull_class === 'Sulcus-Mk3').length

<Scene stamp="MET 027/14:00" location="Bridge · intel terminal · 0.28 AU from Callisto, 4 mgee" condensed="You draft the preliminary: two processes among the losses, one abrupt and coded piracy, one gradual and coded unknown, both late in the Lane. Ferrier reads every display before it goes out under your name.">

You write it the way you wrote the *Asgard* report twenty years ago, in the order a hostile reader will read it. Thirty-one losses in 2,200 records. What the file is a sample of. The classification table, by office. The shape of time-to-silence, which is two shapes. Mark at last contact against the Board's own flat axis. Age, value. The contact log, sixty plumes, one of them hot for its declared mass, its certification requested and not yet answered.

You do not write *piracy*. You do not write *Sulcus*. You write that the incident file, read as a distribution rather than a mean, shows two processes; that one of them is abrupt and coded piracy, the other gradual and coded unknown; that both are concentrated late in the Lane; and that the lost hulls' declared cargo is cheaper than the Lane's, which is not what piracy looks like. Not an accusation. A shape.

Four paragraphs. Eleven displays. Ferrier takes the slate to the intel terminal and opens the file beside it.

</Scene>

<Dialogue speaker="xo" aside="Not looking up.">
This goes out under the Captain's name. I want every display in it to survive a hostile reader. Assuming the file is complete, and assuming they read it — which they will, once, looking for the line to strike.
</Dialogue>

<Dialogue speaker="xo">
So I'll strike first. Each one, from the numbers. Say it back to me.
</Dialogue>

<Dialogue speaker="analyst" aside="He has been waiting to say it since the reply came in.">
So — sir. The gradual pile. The {gradual} hulls that faded over hours instead of going dark in a minute. I pulled their hull classes. All {sulcusGradual} of them are Sulcus-class, Mk 3 drives, Sulcus Freight. Every one passed Bureau certification at Uruk High — declared mass, fuel load, examiner's stamp. And the one plume we flagged is a Sulcus hull running hot for its declared mass.
</Dialogue>

<Dialogue speaker="xo">
Hot is an adjective, Ensign. Heavy is a number. We don't have it.
</Dialogue>

<Dialogue speaker="analyst">
No, sir. But the Lane does. The relay net logs every hull's state vector from Mark 2. If a hull is heavier than its certificate says, its acceleration under a known drive is lower than it should be — thrust over mass. The Bureau has the declared mass. The relay net has the acceleration. Put them side by side for every departure and the ones that lied are the ones off the line.
</Dialogue>

<Scene stamp="MET 030/10:05" condensed="Two variables, not one. You draft the request to the Bureau of Hulls and Cargo for departure certifications, and to the Authority for the Mark 2 telemetry.">

Two variables, not one. Twenty years ago you would have written the request in an hour. You write it in forty minutes, and you address it through the Directorate of Lane Operations, as directed, and you copy nobody, because you have not yet been given a reason to.

The ship leans four milligee toward Ceres. The file stays open on the intel terminal, thirty-one rows of it, and Ebele's histograms, which he is no longer proud of, and which are, all of them, correct.

</Scene>
