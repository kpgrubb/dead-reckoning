<Briefing title="A table and its denominator" topics={["1.1", "1.3"]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
A **frequency table** counts how many individuals fall in each category of one categorical variable. Divide each count by the total and you have **relative frequencies** — proportions that sum to one. The table is honest exactly as far as its **denominator** is: change what goes in the total and every proportion moves, whether or not anything in the world has.

Then the harder question: what **population** does the table describe? A table describes the individuals whose rows went into it — no more. The Register's rows are *incidents as recorded and classified by the Lane Authority's two offices*. That is what any table built from it speaks for. It does not speak for hulls, or for transits, or for the Lane: a transit that filed nothing has no row. The Register is not the population. It is a recorded sample of a reporting process, and the process has two offices in it.

A **count** and a **proportion** answer different questions. *Thirty-one* says how many. *One point four percent* says how many per hundred of some denominator — and is meaningless until you have said which.
</Briefing.Intuition>
<Briefing.Definition>
<Formula tex="\text{relative frequency of category } k = \frac{n_k}{n}" terms={{ 'n_k': 'count of individuals in category k', 'n': 'total individuals in the table — the denominator' }} label="Relative frequency" />

The relative frequencies of every category of one variable sum to 1. A **population** is every individual a question is about; a **sample** is the subset actually recorded. The population a table describes is the set of rows it was built from — after every filter.
</Briefing.Definition>
<Briefing.Worked>
Your own 2176 patrol report from the cutter *Asgard* — the one the Board prefers to forget — was a frequency table with two rows. Transits observed on the Lane, 2168–76: {asgardReport.transits.toLocaleString('en-US')}. Of those, lost: {asgardReport.lost}; arrived: {(asgardReport.transits - asgardReport.lost).toLocaleString('en-US')}.

Relative frequency of a loss: {asgardReport.lost} ÷ {asgardReport.transits.toLocaleString('en-US')} = **{fmt(asgardReport.lost / asgardReport.transits, 4)}**, about {fmt((100 * asgardReport.lost) / asgardReport.transits, 2)} per hundred transits. Arrived: {fmt((asgardReport.transits - asgardReport.lost) / asgardReport.transits, 4)}. The two sum to one. The denominator was *transits observed by one cutter over eight years* — and the report said so in its first line, because a proportion without its denominator is a rumour.
</Briefing.Worked>
<Briefing.Caution>
*"The Register is the population — it's everything that happened on the Lane."* It is everything two offices wrote down. And on the *Asgard* page above, *"{asgardReport.lost} hulls"* and *"{fmt((100 * asgardReport.lost) / asgardReport.transits, 2)} percent of transits"* are one fact in two forms; treating a count as a share, or a share as a count, is how a summary page hides how few or how many a number really is.
</Briefing.Caution>
</Briefing>
