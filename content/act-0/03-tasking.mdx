<Briefing title="A table and its denominator" topics={["1.1", "1.3"]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
A **frequency table** counts how many individuals fall into each category of one categorical variable. Divide each count by the table's total and you have **relative frequencies** — proportions of the whole, which sum to 1 (or to 100 %).

The total you divide by is the **denominator**, and it is the part of the table most easily got wrong. Every relative frequency in the table depends on it. Filter the rows first and the denominator changes, so every proportion changes, whether or not anything in the world has changed.

A **count** and a **proportion** answer different questions. *Thirty-one* says how many. *One point four percent* says how many per hundred of some total — and it means nothing until that total is named. Converting between them needs the denominator: count = proportion × total.

Finally, the question the numbers cannot answer by themselves: what **population** does the table describe? A table describes exactly the individuals whose rows went into it, after every filter — no more. A register of reported incidents describes reported incidents. It does not describe the journeys on which nothing was reported, because those have no row. Absent is not the same as zero.
</Briefing.Intuition>
<Briefing.Definition>
<Formula tex="\text{relative frequency of category } k = \frac{n_k}{n}" terms={{ 'n_k': 'count of individuals in category k', 'n': 'total individuals in the table — the denominator' }} label="Relative frequency" />

The relative frequencies of all categories of one variable sum to 1. A **population** is every individual a question is about; a **sample** is the subset actually recorded. The population a table describes is the set of rows it was built from.
</Briefing.Definition>
<Briefing.Worked>
A patrol report from the cutter *Asgard* covering 2168–76 is a frequency table with two rows. Individuals: transits observed. Total: {asgardReport.transits.toLocaleString('en-US')}. Lost: {asgardReport.lost}. Arrived: {asgardArrived.toLocaleString('en-US')}.

*Relative frequencies.* Lost: {asgardReport.lost} ÷ {asgardReport.transits.toLocaleString('en-US')} = **{fmt(asgardReport.lost / asgardReport.transits, 4)}**, or {fmt((100 * asgardReport.lost) / asgardReport.transits, 2)} per hundred transits. Arrived: {asgardArrived.toLocaleString('en-US')} ÷ {asgardReport.transits.toLocaleString('en-US')} = **{fmt(asgardArrived / asgardReport.transits, 4)}**. The two sum to 1, which is the check.

*Back to a count.* {fmt(asgardReport.lost / asgardReport.transits, 4)} × {asgardReport.transits.toLocaleString('en-US')} = {asgardReport.lost}. The proportion and the count carry the same fact only while the denominator travels with them.

*Population.* This table describes transits observed by one cutter over eight years. It does not describe the Lane, and it does not describe transits nobody watched.
</Briefing.Worked>
<Briefing.Caution>
**Mistaking the record for the world.** "The Register is everything that happened on the Lane." It is everything two offices wrote down. A journey that filed nothing contributes no row, so it cannot appear in any denominator built from the file.

**Reading a count as a share, or a share as a count.** "{asgardReport.lost} hulls" and "{fmt((100 * asgardReport.lost) / asgardReport.transits, 2)} percent of transits" are the same fact in two forms. Quoted without its denominator, either one can be made to sound large or small at will.

**Filtering before counting.** Apply a filter and the total shown is the filter's total, not the file's. If the question is about the whole file, the denominator is the whole file.
</Briefing.Caution>
</Briefing>

<Calculator.Step keys={['STAT', '1:Edit']}>Type the category counts into **L1**, one per row — every category in the table, not just the one you care about.</Calculator.Step>
<Calculator.Step keys={['2nd', 'MODE']}>QUIT to the home screen.</Calculator.Step>
<Calculator.Step keys={['2nd', 'LIST', '▸ MATH', '5:sum(']} note="sum(L1)">The denominator, computed rather than copied. `L1` is `2nd` `1`.</Calculator.Step>
<Calculator.Read label="sum(L1)" value={String(asgardReport.transits)}>the total the whole table is divided by.</Calculator.Read>
<Calculator.Step keys={['STO▸', '2nd', '2']} note="L1/sum(L1) STO▸ L2">Build the whole expression on the home screen, then store it. Dividing a list by a number divides every entry, so one line converts the entire column of counts into relative frequencies. `STO▸` is the key above `ON`.</Calculator.Step>
<Calculator.Step keys={['STAT', '1:Edit']}>Read the proportions down **L2**, or pull a single one on the home screen with `L2(1)`.</Calculator.Step>
<Calculator.Read label="L2(1)" value={fmt(asgardReport.lost / asgardReport.transits, 6)}>the relative frequency of the first category.</Calculator.Read>
<Calculator.Watch>**Check `sum(L2)` reads 1.** If it does not, a category is missing from L1 or one has been entered twice, and every proportion in L2 is wrong.</Calculator.Watch>
<Calculator.Watch>**The wrong total is the standard trap.** Typing only the categories you are interested in makes `sum(L1)` their subtotal, and every proportion comes out too large. If the question asks for a fraction of the whole file, every row of the file has to be in L1 — including the categories the question never mentions.</Calculator.Watch>
<Calculator.Watch>Going the other way — proportion back to a count — is `L2 × sum(L1)`, and only if `sum(L1)` is the same total the proportion was built from. A percentage of the wrong total returns a plausible number of records that do not exist.</Calculator.Watch>
</Calculator>
