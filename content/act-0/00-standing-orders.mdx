<Scene stamp="2184 · standing orders" location="Valhalla, Callisto" condensed="You are Commander Anselm Rook of the Compact Orbital Service. You have the Compact's only stealth corvette and orders to watch a trade corridor where thirty-one ships have gone missing in six years. The official finding is accidents and pirates. There are no witnesses. There is a file.">

Jupiter has four settled moons and nine million people. They call themselves the Galilean Compact. Ganymede holds the parliament and the water. Callisto holds the naval yards and a rock called Valhalla, with the Admiralty Board inside it and eleven hundred officers under it. Europa mines ice. Io mines sulphur and pays triple.

The Compact is small. Mars is not, and neither is Earth. Both of them burn helium-3 scooped out of Jupiter's upper atmosphere at a price the Compact sets, and neither of them likes paying it. You have been in the Service thirty-four years and that argument has outlasted every ship you served in.

Everything the Compact sells travels the same road. Jovian orbit to Ceres is a hundred days under a freighter's torch, with twelve transponder marks along the way. The crews call it the Hundred-Day Lane. Two and a half thousand transits have run it in six years.

Thirty-one of those ships never arrived.

The Lane Authority keeps a file on every incident on the corridor, {fmtInt(register.length)} records deep. Its finding on the losses is that space is large and machinery fails, and that pirates have lately moved into the Belt end. Nobody argued. The insurers paid and the families were told.

You are Anselm Rook. Fifty-three years old, with three ships behind you. The last two years you spent ashore, as the Service's liaison to the yard that built the thing you are about to take out of the slip.

*Nightjar* is sixty-two metres of hull wrapped around sixty tonnes of solid lithium. Every warship radiates. Reactors are hot, crews are hot, and in the dark between moons heat is the only thing anyone can see from a million kilometres out. *Nightjar* pumps her heat inward into the lithium instead of out through the wings. While the metal has capacity left she is a cold rock on a ballistic path. When it fills she runs her radiators out where anyone watching will see them, or she cooks. The Chief will tell you how many hours you have, and she will put a plus-or-minus on the figure.

Your orders are one page and the verb is *observe*. Take her down the Lane, watch, and report what the corridor looks like to a ship nobody can see.

There are no witnesses to any of the thirty-one. No wreckage recovered. No distress call logged. No survivor. What there is, is {fmtInt(register.length)} records with dates and positions and cause codes in them, and {fmtInt(losses.length)} rows where a ship stops.

Everything you find, you are going to find in numbers. Nobody is going to tell you.

</Scene>

<Panel label="ORIENTATION · NAMES YOU WILL NEED" tone="log">

Nothing here needs memorising. The names recur, and this page stays where it is.

**Where**
- **The Galilean Compact** — the four Jovian moons, your country. Capital Uruk, on Ganymede.
- **Valhalla** — the naval seat on Callisto. The **Admiralty Board** sits there; its transmissions are signed by a Flag Secretary and arrive late.
- **Adlinda** — the Callisto yard that built your ship.
- **The Hundred-Day Lane** — the trade corridor from the Jovian system to Ceres, marked 1 to 12. Ceres is Belt territory and not yours.
- **The Lane Authority** — the civilian body that runs the corridor and keeps the incident **Register**. It classifies a loss from whichever office covers the flag: Uruk for Compact hulls, Ceres for everyone else.

**Who**
- **You** — Commander Anselm Rook, Compact Orbital Service, commanding **CSV *Nightjar***.
- **Ferrier**, your executive officer. She will ask what you would expect to see if you were wrong.
- **Sandoval**, chief engineer. Will not give you a figure without a plus-or-minus on it.
- **Oyelaran**, sensors. Trusts the instruments further than they deserve.
- **Ebele**, intelligence analyst. Three years out of the academy, faster than he is careful.

**What the ship can do**
Run cold, on a stored-heat budget measured in hours, then purge. Nothing else here is exotic: no faster-than-light, no artificial gravity, no invented physics. Signals take as long as light takes, so a reply from Valhalla is never sooner than the round trip.

</Panel>

<Panel label="ORIENTATION · HOW THIS WORKS" tone="intel">

Each module runs in the same order, and the two registers are kept apart on purpose.

- **The scene** puts you somewhere and raises a question. It never teaches.
- **The briefing** answers it in plain language: the idea, the notation, a worked case, and the mistake people make. This is the statistics, stated straight.
- **On the calculator** gives the TI-84 Plus CE keystrokes where a procedure exists, including the trap on that screen.
- **The instrument** is yours to move. Drag the points, change *n*, run the simulation.
- **Drills** are generated fresh each time, so a retry is new numbers rather than a remembered answer.
- **The mission call** is the scene again, waiting on your analysis. Get it wrong and somebody tells you which method you reached for; the story does not branch, and nothing is lost.

Modules unlock in order. To move around freely while you are finding your feet, turn off *Lock modules until prerequisites are complete* in **Settings**.

</Panel>
