import { mean } from '@/lib/stats'

export const fixes = [11.2, 14.8, 9.6, 13.1, 12.4, 15.9]

<Scene stamp="MET 000:04:12" location="Callisto departure corridor" condensed="You take the corvette out on her first shakedown. The navigation officer reports dead-reckoning fix errors; you ask for the numbers, not the adjectives.">

The corvette answers the helm like something that has been waiting a long time to be let out. You keep your hands off the console and watch the officers work.

Forward of you, the navigation station is logging position fixes against the Callisto beacon — dead reckoning first, beacon truth second, the difference in kilometers. The XO reads the errors off the display and calls them "acceptable."

You ask for the numbers instead.

</Scene>

<Dialogue speaker="xo" aside="Not looking up from the plot.">
Six fixes since departure, Commander. Errors between about ten and sixteen kilometers. Nothing to worry about.
</Dialogue>

<Briefing title="The mean as a summary" topics={[]}>
<Briefing.Intuition>
"Between ten and sixteen" is a range. A range hides where most of the values sit. The first thing you want from a handful of numbers is a single number that stands for all of them — a **center**.
</Briefing.Intuition>
<Briefing.Definition>
<Formula tex="\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i" terms={{ '\\bar{x}': 'sample mean', 'n': 'number of observations', 'x_i': 'the i-th observation' }} label="Sample mean" />
</Briefing.Definition>
<Briefing.Worked>
The six fix errors are {fixes.join(', ')} km. Their sum is {fixes.reduce((a, b) => a + b, 0).toFixed(1)}; divided by six, the mean is **{mean(fixes).toFixed(2)} km**.
</Briefing.Worked>
</Briefing>

<Plot spec={{ kind: 'dotplot', values: fixes, label: 'Fix error (km)' }} label="NAV · DEAD-RECKONING FIX ERROR" description="Dotplot of six dead-reckoning fix errors in kilometers, ranging from about 9.6 to 15.9." showTable />
