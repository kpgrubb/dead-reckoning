  concept="Chi-square procedures compare counts in categories against the counts a model expects. Goodness of fit tests one categorical variable against a claimed distribution with expected counts n·pᵢ and df = k − 1; a two-way table tests two variables with expected counts (row total × column total)/n and df = (r − 1)(c − 1), and whether it is called independence or homogeneity is settled by the sampling design and not by the arithmetic, which is identical. Every chi-square P-value is the upper tail alone, because any departure in any direction makes the statistic larger, and rejecting says the distribution differs somewhere, never that every cell does — the cell contributions are what name where. Two questions have no test as their answer: when an expected count falls below five the approximation is not trustworthy and the null is simulated instead, and when the data are the whole population there is nothing to infer to and the table is described."
